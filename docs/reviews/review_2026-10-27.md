# Review of the current ESC-50 research

27 October 2026. Decision: continue the project through baseline validation. The current evidence establishes a reproducible development pilot. The question of whether recurrent aggregation improves useful environmental-sound classification remains open.

Scope: inspection of source code, full run histories, checkpoints and saved per-clip predictions; review-time metric recalculation and bounded checkpoint inference on training/validation data. Two agents reviewed methodology and implementation separately. No new optimization, test inference, GPU allocation or implementation change occurred. Agent review does not constitute external replication.

## What currently holds up

Pinned dataset/code, explicit source-ID auditing, train-only fitted normalization, validation-based checkpoint selection and retained per-clip predictions form a credible engineering starting point. The independent implementation reviewer reran all eight tests and checked disjoint source-ID sets, checkpoint normalization, and raw-WAV → cached-feature → saved-prediction consistency for one validation clip per model. These checks found no defect invalidating the current CPU pilot scores.

The source-overlap finding is useful. It is an audit of metadata dependencies, not proof of identical acoustic content or a novel algorithm. Strict development has 1,200 training, 398 validation and 400 test clips. Acoustic near-duplicate or segment-overlap detection remains unperformed. The [official dataset repository](https://github.com/karolpiczak/ESC-50) defines five-fold benchmarking; the modified development split must keep its own label.

## Findings in priority order

**R1 — High: the experiment stops before it can answer the main question.** Both Colab validation curves improve between epochs 1 and 2: CNN accuracy 6.03% → 14.07%, CRNN 9.05% → 12.81%; validation loss also falls. Two epochs/76 updates establish early optimization and cost, without establishing plateau quality. The CNN–CRNN ordering even reverses between these two epochs. Evidence: `results/historical/colab_evidence/runs/*/history.json`. Action: predeclare a longer common maximum-epoch budget and selection rule, retain every epoch and failure, and inspect learning curves before choosing a family.

**R2 — High: the architecture comparison cannot identify a recurrence mechanism.** CRNN has 79,778 parameters against CNN's 26,658 (2.99×). Capacity, temporal processing, initialization of the added head and optimization change together. Equal data exposure is a legitimate recipe comparison, but cannot attribute a future gain to order-sensitive processing. Action after a stable baseline: compare a parameter-comparable nonrecurrent head, then permute encoder time tokens with fixed permutations. Token shuffling tests dependence on order and introduces distribution shift; it does not by itself prove that exploiting order improves generalization. Current models are established families; no novelty or exact paper reproduction has been demonstrated.

**R3 — High: there is no uncertainty evidence for the five-clip ranking.** Recomputed Colab correctness: both models correct on 25 clips, CNN alone on 31, CRNN alone on 26, both wrong on 316. Hence 56 versus 51 correct is five net clips among 57 disagreements. One seed/fold gives no estimate of retraining variation. Running that seed on two different hardware/software environments does not supply independent seeds. Neither superiority nor equivalence is supported. Action: after the larger pilot, define repetition needs and a minimum meaningful gain/cost criterion; report every attempted configuration, fold and seed. Do not treat correlated clips or five folds as independent datasets.

**R4 — High for usability: most classes currently have zero measured recall.** CNN predicts 24/50 classes and recognizes at least one clip in 15/50; CRNN predicts 30/50 and recognizes at least one in 18/50. Thus zero recall affects 35 and 32 classes respectively. Only 7–8 clips/class remain in validation, so individual class estimates are coarse. These are useful failure signals, not proof that the architectures cannot solve those classes. Action: inspect per-class errors and real audio, and verify a small real-audio subset can be memorized before introducing more architecture or augmentation choices.

**R5 — Medium: the original logs lack comparable training diagnostics.** Online training loss is averaged while weights change and dropout/BatchNorm are in training mode; it is not the same measurement as final-checkpoint validation loss. Review-time evaluation of the saved local checkpoints gives CNN training accuracy 14.75% / validation 14.57%, CRNN training 16.25% / validation 13.57%. Both have low training accuracy; this weakens a simple severe-overfitting explanation. Early underfitting is plausible, but optimization, representation and train/eval behavior remain competing explanations. Action: log evaluation-mode training accuracy/loss at each epoch, retaining online loss separately. Review calculations are in `results/historical/review_diagnostics.json` and are not Colab results.

**R6 — Medium: the objective and confirmation boundary need tightening.** “Improvement enough to justify cost” has no declared minimum meaningful gain or cost ceiling. “Robust and efficient” in the brief also lacks a noise condition, latency workload or resource target. Action: declare the initial estimand as a fixed-data-exposure comparison, state a practically meaningful gain and acceptable cost, and report other resource dimensions separately. Preserve fold 5 for the current final confirmation. Folds 1–4 have already informed development; later rotating all five folds must not imply five newly untouched confirmations after global recipe choices. A genuinely nested study must repeat applicable selection steps inside each outer training partition; a conventional fixed-recipe benchmark should disclose its development history.

**R7 — Medium: literature checks justify a starting direction, not a competitive baseline.** The records honestly label abstract/bibliographic checks and unavailable full text. Before claiming a literature-grounded baseline, read one directly relevant ESC-50 method's full preprocessing, architecture, training and evaluation protocol and reproduce a clearly bounded aspect. A simple statistical-feature classifier would also help judge whether the small CNN provides value. The [Salamon–Bello paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04363) motivates studying representation, augmentation and capacity jointly; its numerical result is not our ESC-50 result. Do not add attention or transformers merely because the current two-epoch accuracy is low.

## Implementation issues before expanding the experiment

1. **Cache/checkpoint compatibility (P2):** `esc50.py:276` loads cache arrays without checking the sidecar's frontend, digest or schema. Training stamps the current global frontend into a new checkpoint, and `predict` uses the current global frontend rather than validating the checkpoint's one. The reviewer demonstrated acceptance of an inconsistent sidecar. Current cache identity was independently checked, so current results stand. Before frontend ablations, validate array alignment/shape/dtype, cache digest and preprocessing compatibility; instantiate the stored frontend or reject incompatible checkpoints.
2. **Portable evaluation provenance (P2):** checkpoint payload at `esc50.py:392` omits source policy and repaired split membership/manifest identity. A detached checkpoint cannot establish strict versus official training from fold numbers alone. Store a manifest identifier/hash and source policy, and carry them into final-test artifacts.
3. **Experiment-status audit (P2):** `evaluate` does not update the originating run's `test_evaluated=False`; output files can overwrite an existing evaluation directory. A Boolean training snapshot cannot serve as a lifetime test-access audit. Add an append-only evaluation record and prevent silent replacement before final testing. Detected hardware and actual selected execution device also need separate fields before CPU/GPU comparisons.

These are actionable future-experiment risks, not evidence that the saved current CPU scores are incorrect. No implementation repairs were made during this review.

## The next bounded research cycle

1. Add the compatibility/provenance checks and evaluation-mode training readout. Verify real-audio tiny-subset optimization. Keep test predictions sealed.
2. Add a cheap feature-statistics classifier as a diagnostic anchor. Then predeclare a longer CNN/CRNN development run using the same data exposure and checkpoint opportunities. Set the wall-time safety cap high enough that equal-epoch runs finish; if it cuts a run short, report actual exposure and do not describe budgets as equal.
3. Use those curves to decide whether to repair the baseline, increase the training budget, or study a specific error. Estimate seed variation before choosing repetitions. Freeze the practical gain/cost target before the confirmatory comparison.
4. Once stable, add the nonrecurrent capacity control and temporal-order ablation. Make noise robustness or inference-efficiency claims only with separately specified measurements. Freeze the selected recipe before current holdout evaluation, and describe broader cross-validation with the correct development boundary.

The next acceptance decision is whether a stable, correctly measured baseline exists. A higher score alone is insufficient if it comes from a changed split, more search, more training exposure or undocumented preprocessing.
